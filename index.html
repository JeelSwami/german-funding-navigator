One programme, two very different variants

The Walter Benjamin Position funds a salaried postdoc job in Germany. The DFG pays your host institution, the host employs you, and you get a normal German employment contract with pension, health, and unemployment insurance included. The 2026 funding rate for a postdoc position is €88,200 per year in gross employer costs, oriented on pay scale TV-L E13 to E14. DFG 60.12 DFG 52.18

The Walter Benjamin Fellowship funds the same idea abroad, but as a personal stipend paid directly to you: a basic amount of €2,188 per month in the DFG's current award calculator, plus a country dependent foreign allowance, cost of living compensation, and child allowances. A stipend is not employment. No employer pays into your pension or unemployment insurance, and you arrange health insurance yourself. DFG 52.19 DFG calculator

Both variants add a flat €313 per month for direct project costs, travel, and publications, and up to €5,000 per year for book format publications. Everything else, from lab space to consumables beyond the flat rate, comes from your host. You can also combine phases, for example a year abroad on the fellowship followed by a year in Germany on the position, planned month by month at proposal time. DFG 50.10

The rule international researchers most often misread

The direction of travel decides everything. The Position in Germany is open to researchers anywhere in the world, of any nationality, with no prior connection to Germany. If you did your PhD in India, the US, or anywhere else and want to bring your project to a German lab, this is your entry point. DFG 50.10 §3.1

The Fellowship abroad works the other way: it sends people who are already part of the German research system out into the world. You qualify through one of two routes. Either you completed most of your schooling and university education in Germany, and since the doctorate you have spent under three years abroad for research and no more than one year at your chosen host. Or you have worked in Germany as a researcher continuously for the last three years, in which case you also submit the proposal from Germany. It is a common and painful surprise, so check this rule first if you were hoping to be funded outside Germany. DFG 50.10 §3.1

Are you in the window?

  • You need a completed doctorate, but you can already apply once your thesis is submitted to the examinations office. Include the thesis, proof of submission, and a short statement from your supervisor. Money only flows after you pass all exams. DFG 50.10
  • There is no fixed "years since PhD" limit. The DFG asks whether you are still in the early postdoctoral phase, read per discipline, and counts parental leave in your favour. You stop being eligible once you have already run your own projects, managed staff, or led a group. DFG FAQ DFG 50.10
  • Mobility is a core expectation: the point is a new academic environment. Staying at your current institution needs a specific justification, such as family care or facilities that exist nowhere else, plus a genuinely new research direction. DFG 50.10
  • Hosts must be research institutions that allow you to publish; in Germany they are normally non profit. If your German host is a Max Planck, Fraunhofer, or Helmholtz institute, it has to co finance 45 percent of the position itself, so raise this with them early. DFG 50.10

What reviewers are told to look for

Reviewers assess three things: you (qualifications, potential, career trajectory), the project (originality and international level), and the fit of your chosen host for this particular project and this stage of your career. Recommendation letters are not allowed; the proposal has to carry the argument on its own. DFG 10.219

After the fellowship: the return grant

If you spend at least 12 months abroad on the fellowship, you can apply for a return grant of up to six months to come back and reconnect with the German research system, by a simple letter to the DFG staff who handled your award. DFG 52.19

What you actually get

An Emmy Noether award makes you a group leader with your own budget line: your own position (the 2026 DFG rate is €102,300 per year, oriented between pay scales E14 and E15), plus the doctoral researchers, postdocs, equipment, and running costs your project needs. The DFG sets no upper funding limit; you request what the science justifies. DFG 60.12 DFG FAQ

Just as important is the legal standing. The DFG asks your host university to sign a model contract giving you the effective rights of a junior professor: you supervise doctoral researchers, you teach two hours a week per semester, and you answer for your own research. After year three an evaluation checks your progress and whether the university actually honoured those rights; pass it and the second three years are released. DFG 50.02 DFG 10.223

Are you in the window?

  • Submit within four years of your doctorate. Licensed physicians and psychologists get six. The clock runs from your certificate date if it was issued within a year of your defense, otherwise from the defense itself. DFG 50.02 DFG FAQ
  • Children extend the window: two years per child for women, one year per child for men (two with documented care beyond a year), capped at six extra years in total, for children under 12 living in your household. DFG 50.02
  • You need roughly two years of postdoc experience first, in the DFG's words "as a rule". So the realistic application zone is years two to four after your PhD. DFG 50.02
  • Substantial international research experience is required. Time abroad is the obvious evidence, but international collaborations or an international working environment during your PhD or postdoc in Germany also count. You describe it in a separate appendix. DFG 50.02
  • You are excluded if you already meet the requirements for a professorship appointment, or if you already lead a comparable independent junior research group. DFG 50.02
  • Your group should not sit at the university that awarded your PhD; if it does, the proposal must justify why. DFG 50.02

Applying from outside Germany

This programme was explicitly designed to pull outstanding researchers from abroad into the German system, and it checks career stage, not citizenship. The group itself must be based in Germany. If you are not yet part of the German research system, you add one document: a written confirmation that you intend to continue your research career in Germany after the funding ends. You can name several candidate host universities in one proposal and pick the final one within two months of the award. DFG 50.02

Two traps to know before you sign

  • If you accept a professorship in Germany during the grant, your project money continues but your own salary line ends. If you leave the German research system entirely, the whole grant ends. Plan your exit routes accordingly. DFG 50.02
  • Funding has to start being used within six months of approval, so do not apply before you can realistically move. DFG FAQ

The 2027 call is unusual, in your favour

Normally you can apply between 2 and 7 years after your PhD defense. For the 2027 call the ERC widened this to more than 0 and at most 10 years before 1 January 2027, meaning a defense between 1 January 2017 and 31 December 2026 qualifies. Researchers who thought they had aged out of the Starting Grant get one more shot this year. A career cap arrives with it: from 2027 you can hold at most one Starting and one Consolidator Grant in a lifetime. WP 2027 ERC news

The clock runs from the date of your successful defense, not your certificate, and your first doctorate counts if you have more than one. The window stretches for documented life events: a flat 18 months per child for maternity, the leave actually taken for paternity, plus documented long term illness, national service, and clinical training up to four years. The extensions add up with no overall cap. Info for Applicants

Anyone, from anywhere

Nationality and current residence do not matter; researchers of any age from anywhere in the world can apply, and a PhD from any country counts. What matters is where the work happens: your host must be in an EU member state or a country associated to Horizon Europe, you must spend at least half your total working time in Europe, and at least half your working time on the project. The UK and Switzerland are both fully associated again, so hosts there qualify too. WP 2027 EU country list

If you are based outside the EU and associated countries at the deadline and would relocate to take up the grant, you can request up to €2 million on top of the core budget instead of the usual €1 million, bringing the theoretical maximum to €3.5 million. The ERC keeps a dedicated page for researchers moving in from third countries. ERC for non-European researchers

Germany is the strongest host country by volume: 99 of the 478 Starting Grants in the 2025 round went to researchers at German institutions, about one in five. ERC statistics 2025

How the competition works

  • One deadline per year. You submit the full proposal at once: Part B1 with a five page scientific case plus a four page CV and track record, and Part B2 with the seven page detailed proposal. Info for Applicants
  • Step 1: one of 28 field panels reads only B1. Top scored proposals ("A invited") move on. Step 2: the panel reads everything and interviews you, roughly 30 minutes, remotely. For the 2027 call, Step 1 results are expected 11 May 2027, interviews in June, final results 24 August 2027, and grant signature around December 2027. Info for Applicants
  • A binding host support letter is mandatory at submission. It guarantees your independence: your own budget authority, your own team, publishing as senior author, and space. Universities need internal lead time, so contact the research office one to two months before the deadline at the latest. WP 2027
  • Rejection has consequences: a C in Step 1 in the 2025 or 2026 calls, or a B in Step 1 in the 2026 calls, blocks you from all 2027 main calls. A B in Step 2 carries no ban. Budget for the possibility of one resubmission when you plan your timing. ERC news
  • The grant is portable: if you move to another eligible institution, the grant can move with you. Some universities court ERC winners with tenure track offers for exactly this reason. WP 2027

The two clocks that decide everything

Questions researchers actually ask

Honest answers, with receipts

Collected from the official DFG and ERC FAQ pages and from the questions international researchers keep asking on forums like Academia Stack Exchange. Every answer links to where it comes from.

I did my PhD outside Germany and have never worked there. Which of these can I even apply for?

Three of the four doors are open. The Walter Benjamin Position (in Germany) has no nationality or prior Germany requirement. Emmy Noether explicitly invites applications from abroad; you just add a statement of intent to stay in Germany afterwards. The ERC never cared where you or your PhD come from. The one closed door is the Walter Benjamin Fellowship abroad, which requires existing integration into the German research system.

DFG Walter Benjamin FAQ · DFG form 50.02 · ERC non-European researchers

Does my citizenship or country of origin matter at all?

For eligibility, no. None of the three programmes checks your passport; they check career stage and where the work will happen. Two practical caveats: EU sanctions exclude host institutions established in Russia, Belarus, and non government controlled territories of Ukraine (individual researchers of those nationalities based elsewhere remain eligible, with screening), and your citizenship does affect the visa procedure for moving to Germany, though not the outcome.

ERC Work Programme 2027, Annex 3 · EURAXESS Germany, visas

Which date starts my eligibility clock?

It depends on the funder, and the difference can decide a borderline case. The ERC counts from the date of your successful defense (first PhD, if you have several). The DFG counts from your certificate date if it was issued within a year of the defense, otherwise from the defense. If your certificate took months to arrive, the DFG clock may start later than the ERC clock, which buys you time with the DFG.

ERC Information for Applicants · DFG Emmy Noether FAQ

Can I apply before my defense?

Walter Benjamin: yes, as soon as your thesis is submitted to the examinations office; include the thesis, proof of submission, and a supervisor statement, and funds flow only after you pass. ERC 2027: your defense must happen by 31 December 2026, which is after the October deadline, so you can apply with a scheduled defense. Emmy Noether expects a completed doctorate plus around two years of postdoc experience, so it is not a pre defense option.

DFG form 50.10 · ERC Work Programme 2027

Stipend or employment contract: what is the real difference in Germany?

An employment contract (Walter Benjamin Position, Emmy Noether, ERC) comes with the full German social package: public health insurance, pension contributions, unemployment insurance, paid vacation. A stipend (Walter Benjamin Fellowship abroad, Humboldt) is usually tax free but builds no pension, no unemployment cover, and you arrange health insurance yourself. Stipend years also tend not to count as "experience" when a later employer sets your TV-L salary step. If the net amounts look similar, the contract is almost always worth more.

EURAXESS Germany, social security · Academia SE discussion

Do I need to speak German?

Not for the applications. DFG proposals can be written in English, and ERC proposals effectively are English. Research groups in the sciences mostly run in English. You will feel the gap in daily life instead: immigration offices, rental contracts, and insurance paperwork are German affairs, and German helps you settle: the EU Blue Card track to permanent residence takes 27 months with basic German, 21 with a B1 certificate. If you plan to stay (which Emmy Noether assumes), start learning.

DFG FAQ · EURAXESS Germany

What are my actual odds?

Emmy Noether runs at roughly 20 percent across all fields by the DFG's own figure. The ERC Starting Grant 2025 call funded 478 of 3,928 proposals, 12.2 percent, and applications rose 22 percent the following year. The DFG does not publish a Walter Benjamin success rate. For all three, the quality of fit between you, the project, and the host moves the odds more than any statistic.

DFG Emmy Noether FAQ · ERC 2025 statistics

What if my chosen university refuses to sign what the funder requires?

It happens, and both funders planned for it. For Emmy Noether you may name several candidate universities in one proposal, and if a university will not sign the model contract you can set up the group elsewhere after consulting the DFG. For the ERC, any eligible institution in the EU or an associated country can be your host; if one research office declines, take the proposal to another. The host support letter is a real commitment, which is exactly why you start that conversation months early.

DFG form 50.02 · Academia SE discussion

Can I take the grant with me if I change institutions?

The ERC grant is famously portable: it belongs to you and moves to any eligible host, which gives you real negotiating power. DFG grants can generally transfer between German institutions with DFG consent, but a Walter Benjamin award fixes the number of funded months per named host at approval, so you cannot switch hosts mid award. Choose carefully the first time.

ERC Work Programme 2027 · DFG Walter Benjamin FAQ

I have children. How do the windows change?

Every programme extends, but by different amounts. ERC: a flat 18 months per child for the mother, and the documented leave actually taken for fathers, with no cap on the total. Emmy Noether: two years per child for women, one for men (two with documented care beyond a year), capped at six extra years. Walter Benjamin has no fixed window, and the DFG explicitly counts parental time in your favour when judging the "early phase". Illness, national service, and clinical training also extend the ERC window.

ERC Work Programme 2027 · DFG form 50.02

Does time in industry disqualify me?

No rule says so. The ERC judges your track record against the calendar window, however you spent it; coming back from a company is possible, though your publication record competes with people who never left academia. The Emmy Noether four year clock keeps running during industry years, which is the bigger constraint. For Walter Benjamin, what matters is whether you are still plausibly in the early postdoc phase.

Academia SE discussion · DFG form 50.02

Can I hold or apply for other funding at the same time?

Within limits. The DFG will not fund a project that another funder is already paying for on the same topic; if two funders say yes, you choose one. You also cannot run an Emmy Noether application in parallel with a DFG research grant that funds your own position. The ERC requires full disclosure of every grant and pending application in the Funding ID, allows at most one main ERC grant at a time, and lets you have only one proposal under evaluation.

DFG form 2.00 §23 (German) · DFG Emmy Noether FAQ · ERC Work Programme 2027

What happens if I get rejected?

With the DFG you can revise and resubmit; there is no formal lockout, and with rolling deadlines your timing is your own. The ERC is stricter: a C at Step 1 in the 2025 or 2026 calls, or a B at Step 1 in the 2026 calls, makes you ineligible for the 2027 calls. A B at Step 2 (reaching the interview but not funded) carries no ban at all. Resubmitted ERC proposals are read fresh, without the old reviews attached.

ERC reapplication rules

Will one of these get me tenure in Germany?

Not directly; the funders fund projects, and tenure in Germany means winning a professorship. But the pathways are real. Emmy Noether exists precisely to make you appointable to a professorship without habilitation, and the interim evaluation checks that your university treats you like a junior professor. An ERC grant is strong leverage, since several universities have standing policies of offering tenure track positions to grantees, who could otherwise carry the grant to a competitor.

DFG form 50.02 · Academia SE discussion

The Walter Benjamin pays a stipend abroad. What should I watch out for?

Three things. First, the payments go only to a German bank account, monthly on the 15th. Second, absences from your host country stop the foreign allowance from day 29, so long trips home have a price. Third, if the United States is your destination, check the J-1 visa's two year home residency rule before you commit; the DFG's own FAQ flags it. Earnings up to €600 per month are not offset against the stipend, and host top ups to the local postdoc level are explicitly allowed.

DFG Walter Benjamin FAQ · DFG form 2.00 §23 (German)

Where can I read successful proposals?

Most winners keep theirs private, but a growing number share them openly; the community maintained Open Grants repository collects examples, including ERC Starting Grants. Your university's research office often keeps an internal collection too, and that office should be your first stop before paying for any preparation course.

The move itself

Visas, insurance, and the fine print of arriving

The grant is only half the move. These are the rules that shape your first year in Germany, from official sources, mainly EURAXESS Germany.

Your residence permit

Funded researchers with a doctorate normally use the researcher permit under §18d of the Residence Act. It works for both employment contracts and fellowships, requires a hosting agreement with a recognised research institution, runs at least a year, and can be extended by up to 18 months for job seeking after the research ends. Spouses and children get residence with full work rights. EURAXESS

If you have an employment contract above €50,700 gross per year (or €45,934 in shortage occupations, which include scientists), the EU Blue Card is the alternative: permanent settlement after 27 months with basic German (A1), or after 21 months with German at B1. A full time TV-L E13 postdoc salary typically clears the shortage threshold. Citizens of Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, the UK, and the US may enter visa free and apply after arrival. EURAXESS

The health insurance trap

On an employment contract you are enrolled in the social system automatically. On a fellowship you are not: no pension, no unemployment insurance, and health insurance is your own job, while remaining a legal condition of the residence permit.

One rule matters more than the rest: researchers arriving on a §18d permit since March 2024 can apply to join statutory health insurance within three months of arrival. Take that window seriously. Choosing private insurance carelessly can lock you out of the statutory system for good, which gets expensive with age and family.

If you later leave Germany for good with under five years of pension contributions, you can usually reclaim your employee share after a 24 month wait; confirm the details with Deutsche Rentenversicherung before counting on it.

Who can host you

Germany has around 430 universities and more than 1,000 public research institutions. The four big non university families split the work roughly like this: Max Planck for curiosity driven basic research, Helmholtz for large infrastructure and grand challenges, Leibniz for everything bridging basic and applied, and Fraunhofer for contract research with industry. All can host DFG funded researchers, with one budget quirk: at Max Planck, Fraunhofer, and Helmholtz institutes, a Walter Benjamin position must be 45 percent co financed by the institute itself. For profit companies cannot host DFG grants.

Adjacent routes worth knowing

Honest one liners, because the right programme is sometimes someone else's.

Humboldt Research Fellowship

For researchers coming from abroad, within 4 years of the PhD: €3,000 per month for 6 to 24 months at a German host of your choice. A stipend, so the insurance caveats apply. Decisions in roughly 3 to 6 months.

humboldt-foundation.de

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships

The EU's mobility fellowship: up to 8 years post PhD, with the rule that you must not have spent more than 12 of the last 36 months in the host country. A real employment contract. Annual calls.

marie-sklodowska-curie-actions.ec.europa.eu

DAAD PRIME

The reverse route: an 18 month German university contract funding 12 months abroad plus 6 months back in Germany. A contract rather than a stipend, with the social insurance that brings.

daad.de

Helmholtz Investigator Groups

Group leadership at a Helmholtz centre with a package of at least €1.75 million over five years, for researchers 2 to 6 years past the PhD with international experience. Run jointly with a partner university.

helmholtz.de

Max Planck Research Groups

The Max Planck Society's own group leader track: W2 level positions, six year contracts with substantial staff and equipment packages, one central call each autumn.

mpg.de
