Three grants quietly decide early research careers in Germany: the DFG Walter Benjamin Programme, the DFG Emmy Noether Programme, and the ERC Starting Grant.

I went through everything official about them. The guideline PDFs, the FAQ pages in German and English, the ERC work programmes, and the questions international researchers keep asking in public forums. Then I built a free interactive guide that puts it all in one place.

Five things that surprised me:

1. The Walter Benjamin Programme runs in two directions. The position in Germany is open to any nationality with zero prior German connection. The fellowship abroad is the opposite: it requires you to already belong to the German research system. People mix these up constantly, and it costs them months.

2. The eligibility clocks differ between funders. The ERC counts from your PhD defense. The DFG counts from your certificate if it arrived within a year of the defense. Same CV, different windows.

3. This year is exceptional for the ERC. The 2027 call (deadline 14 October 2026) widened the window from the usual 2 to 7 years post PhD to 0 to 10. If you thought you had aged out, you have one more chance.

4. A stipend is not a salary. Fellowship money is usually tax free, but it builds no pension, no unemployment cover, and stipend years often do not count toward your future salary step. A contract at the same net amount is nearly always worth more.

5. Nobody checks your passport. All three programmes select on career stage and science. Emmy Noether was explicitly designed to bring researchers from abroad into Germany, and the ERC adds up to 2 million euros in relocation budget if you move in from outside Europe.

The guide has an eligibility navigator that applies the rules to your own dates, side by side comparisons, timelines, document checklists, and every single fact linked to its official source at the DFG, the ERC, and EURAXESS Germany.
